As the last train pulled out of the station, Mia realized she had left her notebook on the bench. Inside were the sketches she’d spent months working on—her dreams of becoming an architect captured in pencil and ink. Panic rose in her chest. She turned, ready to sprint back, but the station gates had already closed for the night. Only the echo of the departing train and the hum of streetlights remained. Defeated, she sat on the curb, watching the city shimmer in the distance. A soft tap on her shoulder startled her. An old man in a conductor’s uniform held out her notebook. “You dropped this,” he said, smiling gently. Mia blinked. “How did you—?” He tipped his hat. “Sometimes, the things we think we’ve lost just take the long way back.” Then he vanished into the shadows. Clutching her notebook, Mia stood, heart lighter. The city didn’t feel quite so lonely anymore.
